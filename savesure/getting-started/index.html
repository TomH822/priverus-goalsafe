Getting Started with SaveSure

SaveSure helps you save steadily for the costs you know are coming, and for the things you hope to do, all in one savings account. Setup takes about ten minutes.

No bank connection. No account to create.

How SaveSure divides your savings

All your money stays in one savings account. SaveSure divides it, on paper, into three parts:

  • Primary Fund — must-pay obligations: amounts you must have ready by a known date, such as property taxes, an insurance premium or an annual registration.
  • Reserve Funds — your goals: a Christmas Club, family birthdays, a vacation, a home-repair cushion. They are filled after the Primary Fund, in the order you rank them.
  • Discretionary Fund — what's left over. Free to spend or move shows what you can use now.

A quick example

Say you have $1,200 in property taxes due in December, $600 of car insurance in March, and a $400 summer trip. You list those three planned withdrawals with their dates, and tell SaveSure how often you add money to savings. SaveSure then shows the steady amount each deposit needs to provide, your Required Deposit, and how much of today's savings to set aside so that all three are covered on time.

Setting up, screen by screen

After the Welcome screen and a short page on how SaveSure divides your savings:

  1. Initial Balance — your savings account's balance today.
  2. Deposits — how much you deposit and how often: weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly or a custom interval.
  3. Planned Withdrawals — each amount you need ready by a known date, and how often it comes due. For a cost that changes every year, add an escalation rate.
  4. Reserve Funds — your goals, ranked 2nd, 3rd and so on (1st priority always belongs to your Planned Withdrawals), each with an amount per deposit and, if you like, a target and planned draws.
  5. Allocations — shown when you have a Reserve Fund and your savings are more than your Required Start: give any Reserve Fund a starting amount from today's balance. What's left goes to Discretionary.
  6. Sufficiency Summary — your Required Deposit compared with your deposit. If your plan starts short, SaveSure shows your choices for catching up.
  7. Alerts — how many days before each planned withdrawal SaveSure reminds you.
  8. Finish — read the SaveSure Disclaimer, tick the box and tap Go to Home.

Using SaveSure day to day

  • Home shows whether you're On Track! and what's coming next.
  • Funds shows each fund's balance, each with its own page; Transfer moves money between funds.
  • Ledger is where you record each deposit and withdrawal. SaveSure never connects to your bank, so you record them yourself.
  • Projections charts your balances into the future.
  • Plan holds your deposit, Planned Withdrawals, Reserve Funds and Initial Balance. Revise setup walks through your plan again, and Settings is at its top.

When you check your bank account, tap Update on Home and enter your Balance at Bank. If they differ, Home shows the difference. On Track! means they match and your plan covers what's coming.

Your privacy

SaveSure does not connect to your bank and does not create an account. What you enter is stored on your iPhone. An Apple backup may include it if you have backups turned on.

Ads

SaveSure is free, with a small banner of non-personalized ads from Google AdMob. A one-time Remove Ads purchase (Plan › Settings › Remove Ads) removes them; Restore Purchase puts it back on a new iPhone.
